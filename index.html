01 // Overview

The Laboratory

TASP is a personal intellectual laboratory for investigating human experience, psychology, philosophy, spirituality, and the ways people construct meaning from what they experience.

It begins with observation: something experienced, noticed, questioned, or repeatedly encountered. From there, an observation becomes a question, the question becomes an investigation, and the investigation develops into a structured body of thought.

"TASP does not present itself as an academic institution or as an authority over the subjects it explores. It is a space for inquiry — where personal observation can become structured thought."

The work takes multiple forms — articles, conceptual investigations, reports, and writing — bound together by a single intent: to observe carefully, question honestly, examine deeply, and understand before concluding.

The Anatomy of a Sane Lie

A lie begins the moment someone speaks against what they already know to be true. Not by accident, not out of confusion — the person knows exactly where the truth stood, and chooses to step away from it anyway. What makes it stranger is what happens next: they don’t call it deception. They quietly rename it, reshape it, until the distance they created starts to feel less like a lie and more like their own private version of “Reality”.

Every deliberate falsehood starts with awareness. Anyone who lies always knows they are stepping away from the truth. But just the presence of a lie doesn’t tell the whole story. The true nature of a deception isn't measured by the words spoken, but by what the lie is actually trying to protect.

Sane Lie vs. Selfish Lie

Sane Lie: We have all stood at that quiet crossroads, wondering if telling the raw truth is actually an act of kindness, or just a wrecking ball. A 'sane lie' isn't about getting an advantage; it comes from a heavy heart. Think of it like a gentle fog dropped over a harsh landscape. It comes from real empathy—the quiet realisation that handing someone the unvarnished truth right now will only shatter something fragile before it's ready to handle it. This isn't trickery or deceit. It's simply recognising that some people just aren't strong enough yet to weather a sudden storm. You bend the truth not to save your own skin, but to act as a shield against a blow that would otherwise crush someone else.

Selfish Lie: On the other side of the coin is the selfish lie, and it wears a completely different face. This isn't built to protect someone else; it's a fortress built entirely around the self. Think of it as a heavy suit of armour worn to dodge accountability, polish a cracked personal image, or run away from consequences before they catch up. Its only job is control—making sure the world sees you exactly how you want to be seen, while hiding the mess underneath. You aren't stepping in front of a storm to shield a friend; you are locking the doors, pulling down the blinds, and leaving someone else out in the cold just to keep your own seat comfortable.

Bridge Between: Living in the Grey

Neither lie alters the fundamental architecture of the truth; the facts remain completely intact whether they are gently shielded behind a curtain of grace or locked away behind walls of self-preservation. Yet, the emotional toll differs entirely based on origin. The ultimate burden of a sane lie is borne quietly within. It is the heavy emotional cost carried by the one who bends reality, knowing they have altered the landscape not for power, but for another's preservation. Conversely, the ultimate cost of a selfish lie is isolation. The fortress built to protect the ego eventually turns into a prison, trapping the liar inside with their own manufactured reflection.

Wisdom, therefore, does not demand that we eliminate all deception with reckless cruelty. Instead, it invites radical self-honesty: When we step away from the raw truth, are we extending a compassionate shield against an unbearable storm, or are we simply bolting the doors to keep ourselves comfortable? The answer defines the true weight of our words.

The God We Construct

"What if the God you have spent your life praying to is not entirely the God you encountered, but partly a construction of your own mind?"

A God shaped by fear, hope, suffering, morality, guilt, desire and expectation; a God who seems generous when life cooperates, becomes a defendant when reality collapses, and is asked to explain why this happened to me. What happens when that construction begins to fracture?

We ask God for outcomes. We thank God for outcomes. We blame God for outcomes. We bargain with God when reality refuses to give us what we believe we deserve. The moment we make God responsible for validating our expectations, we have already created a God who resembles us.

The Illusory Society & "Part-Time Sinners": We construct versions of spirituality that fit comfortably inside our lives — a Dharma that supports us, a Karma that explains other people's suffering, a morality that looks impressive from a distance, and a God who forgives our contradictions. We become “Part-time Sinners.” Not because we consciously wake up intending to be hypocrites, but because human beings are remarkably capable of separating what they know from what they practice.

The Mango Seed: A mango seed does not love or hate you. You plant mango. You cannot demand banana. Perhaps consequence works the same way. We plant resentment and expect peace. Dishonesty and expect trust. Chaos and expect order. Then we ask: “Why me?” The better question is: “What did I plant?”

Surrender is not giving up responsibility. It is accepting its boundary. I am responsible for the seed. I am not sovereign over the forest. I can choose my actions. I cannot command every consequence.

Evidence, Me, or Responsibility

Who am I becoming while I go there?

Creation is a flame, light, and oxygen—elements that altogether define life, with its ultimate symbolism rooted in the divine. Suddenly, the universe asks for your identity. You answer, and the whole cosmos—the sun, the moon, the planets—smiles and whispers, "Weren’t you just a fleeting moment?" But Creation steps in to defend the individual: "I am the individual's identity." We often feel small against the sheer scale of the stars, forgetting that the universe only wakes up to experience itself through the localised consciousness burning inside a human chest.

Gratitude is the key to life, yet in the holy, uninterrupted flow of routine, we forget to say thank you. Pain is not a punishment; it is the universe’s hard reset, stripping away our numbness so we remember how fragile and precious the baseline of being alive truly is. I live not merely for other lives; creating and nurturing other lives is my fundamental responsibility. To put it simply: if I cannot give someone life, I have no right to take it back.

The soul is immortal—neither air, heat, water, nor weapon can destroy it. The pillars of cosmic order frame this eternal journey clearly: Shiva says the birth seeker will surely die, as the physical shell and the ego must burn away; Vishnu says the form will perish, but the Aatmaa never will; and Brahma says the Aatmaa is immortal, but it will inevitably seek birth again, returning to the wheel of creation.

Responsibility, then, isn't a social burden—it is a sacred contract. If we are carrying immortal sparks, our duty is to stop fighting the mirror, honor the flame while it burns in our hands, and transform the battlefield of the mind into a space of deliberate, compassionate creation.

Being Surrounded by People, Yet Being Invisible

"Reality doesn’t speak—it delivers, and most of the time, it delivers emptiness."

There are moments when you are physically present, yet completely absent in people’s attention—sometimes even in your own.

In today’s world, importance is no longer something you naturally hold; it is something you perform, something you earn through perception rather than truth. People feel heard only when they are acknowledged, valued only when they are validated, and somewhere in that process, they forget what they already carry within—their internal spark. The conflict begins when an individual, unsure of who they are, turns to society for answers, forgetting that the same society is still evolving, still uncertain, still driven by its own illusions.

You find yourself sitting in a room full of people, sharing space, sharing time, yet feeling completely disconnected—not because you don’t belong, but because you begin to question if you ever did fit in this space “Not Acceptable Fact for an Individual.”

Insecurities take over, identity begins to blur, and even in a room full of people, you are left with two choices—either become the loudest version of yourself or slowly disappear. Most people don’t even realize when they choose the second. And that is where reality delivers its final outcome—emptiness. You sit with it, silently, questioning not the world but yourself... and yet, somewhere along the way, you stopped being visible to Yourself and to the People who taught you the “Method of Illusion”.

When Love Becomes Identity — And Identity Becomes Emptiness

"Reality doesn’t break you. It reveals what you built on illusion."

There is a difference between loving someone and needing them for emotional stability. Most people don’t realize it when they cross that line. What starts as affection slowly turns into dependence, and before you understand it, the person in front of you is no longer just someone you love—they become the place where you seek peace. And that is where the problem begins. Because peace is not something another person can carry for you.

You think you are loving them deeply, but in reality, you are attaching your sense of self to their presence. You start believing that they are your “life,” when in truth, they were only ever a part of it. A part that was never meant to hold your entire identity.

The truth is harsh, but necessary: Not every truth is meant for you. Some truths exist only to show you where you were wrong. The deeper you feel, the more you start confronting yourself. Not them. Because love doesn’t expose the other person. It exposes you.

At some point, anger fades. Complaints disappear. What remains is something much quieter—fatigue. A deep, internal exhaustion where you are still alive, still functioning, but not really present. The reality is not that they didn’t become yours. The reality is that you handed your entire identity to someone who never saw it as something permanent. And maybe that’s the real loss—not them, but the version of you that depended on them to exist.

Sports Fandom — Becoming a Fan at 21

"I didn’t become a football fan because of a trophy. I became a football fan because of Lionel Messi."

I’ve never been to Argentina. Football was never my favorite sport. The last time I remember playing it seriously was back in Class 5, when I even received a certificate for it. The truth is, until recently, I didn’t even know how many players were on a football team.

Today, for the first time in my life, I watched a football match live—the FIFA World Cup 2026 Final between Argentina and Spain. Then I realized something that made it even more meaningful: I was born in 2004, the same year Lionel Messi made his first-team debut for FC Barcelona. It felt like an unexpected connection between two completely different journeys that started in the same year.

I’m 21 years old, and I’m not embarrassed to say that today is the day I truly became a football fan. Not because Argentina won. Not because of trophies. But because of Lionel Messi.

I don’t want to inherit his fame. I want to inherit his mindset—his discipline, his relentless hard work, his humility, his ability to perform under unimaginable pressure, and the calmness with which he carries both victory and defeat. Tonight, Spain took the trophy. But some victories can never be measured by silverware. Lionel Messi’s legacy, his humility, his discipline, and the way he inspired millions will always be bigger than any final.

I used to think football was an illogical game—22 people just running after a ball for 90 minutes. Today, I realized how wrong I was. Now I see the consistency, the stamina, the discipline, the teamwork, the mental strength, and the relentless effort it takes. Siempre con Argentina. 🇦🇷🐐 Gracias, Leo.
